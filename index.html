What Vietnamese IT employers ask for

On 16 October 2026 I collected 672 live job postings from 320 companies through ITviec's public sitemap, cleaned them into SQLite and queried them. Every number on this page comes from a SQL query in the repository. Four things stood out:

  • 73% of postings never name a salary.
  • English is listed on 190 postings, more than Python (133), the most requested technology.
  • 60% of postings are in Ho Chi Minh City.
  • Advertised pay runs from about $200 a month for interns to $2,065–3,341 for lead roles.

Most postings never say what they pay

Only 183 of 672 postings publish a figure. The other 489 replace it with a slogan such as "You'll love it", so a candidate cannot compare offers before applying. Every salary figure below describes the minority of employers willing to post one, not the market as a whole.

English is listed more often than any technology

English is a listed skill on 190 postings (28%). Python, the most requested technology, appears on 133. Languages are counted apart from technologies below, otherwise the answer to "what is most in demand" would simply be English.

How the data was collected and cleaned

The job listing pages render in JavaScript, so there is nothing to parse in their HTML. Each job page does embed a schema.org JobPosting block for search engines, and the site publishes a sitemap of those pages. The collector checks robots.txt first, makes one request every 1.5 seconds, and can resume after an interruption. Four problems turned up in the data.

The currency field is wrong

Every salary comes labelled as US dollars, including ones whose text is in dong. 24 of the 183 published salaries were really in dong:

Trusting the label would average 14 (million dong) with 1,500 (dollars), so the currency is read from the text instead.

Some dong amounts have no unit

A few postings write 16.000.000- 21.000.000 and nothing else. No job on this board pays tens of thousands of dollars a month, so a figure above a plausibility bound is converted as dong.

Not every tag is a skill

Internship Accepted and Fresher Accepted sit in the same field as Kubernetes. They are left out of skill counts, and spellings such as PostgreSql and Postgres are merged.

An ID that was not unique

The number at the end of each URL looks like a posting ID, but three unrelated postings share "1922". Keying on it silently dropped about fifty jobs, giving 622 instead of 672. The full URL slug is used now.

Limitations

  • One job board, so this describes ITviec's listings rather than every IT vacancy in Vietnam.
  • A snapshot of open postings, not a time series.
  • Salary figures cover only employers who publish one, which biases them toward companies competing openly on pay.
  • Dong is converted at one fixed rate, not the rate on each posting's date.
